[Behind the Phenomenon Series #4] Why Traffic Jams Happen

Drive to the front of a jam and you often find no cause at all.

In this video, the cars at the front only slow from 61 to 45 km/h. But each car behind brakes a little harder to keep its distance, and the one behind that harder still. The reactions stack up until the cars further back crawl along below 20 km/h.

A jam doesn't move forward with the cars. The cars keep moving forward, yet the jam itself grows backward at about 19 km/h. On real highways, jams are observed traveling backward at about 20 km/h. In a 2008 experiment, cars simply driving around a circular track at a steady speed produced a jam with no accident and no hill.

Leave a gap and slow down early and gently. That alone can weaken the wave traveling backward.

*Computed with a traffic-flow model (IDM, 1 s reaction delay): 30 cars per km per lane, 3 lanes. In this video, cars slow slightly on an uphill too gentle to notice.
*The video speeds up to 2x and 3x. On-screen count: cars below 20 km/h, all 3 lanes.
